The speaker

Hujjat al-Islam Mustafa Aminikhah

Mustafa Aminikhah is an Iranian Shiʿi scholar who teaches Qurʾānic exegesis, doctrine and ethics to engineering and science students in Mashhad. His lectures on near-death accounts, Beyond Death, reached Persian speakers around the world.

Education

Born in 1988, Aminikhah studied in the seminaries of Qom and Mashhad up to the advanced level, where students work on exegesis, jurisprudence and legal theory, and read philosophy through Mullā Ṣadrā's Asfār and ʿAllāma Ṭabāṭabāʾī's al-Mīzān. His teachers include Ayatollah Jawādī Āmulī, whom he calls his own. In the lectures he often invokes a mystical lineage that runs through Mullā Ḥusayn-Qulī Hamadānī, Qāḍī and Pahlavān Tihrānī.

Teaching

At Ferdowsi University he is the Leader's representative at the Faculty of Engineering, and engineers and scientists make up most of his audience. He speaks to them informally, with jokes and everyday analogies, a style he adopted to hold students who would not otherwise listen. His website files his talks under the Qurʾān, ethics, hadith commentary, the Ahl al-Bayt, the world of Malakut, doctrine, lifestyle and Mahdism, among other headings, and carries the audio of each one.

Writing and media

Aminikhah co-founded Elm va Farhang high school in Qom. He has written books on near-death accounts, Se daqīqa dar qiyāmat ("Three Minutes in the Resurrection") and Shunūd, as well as Payghambar-e majāzī and Payghambar-e payghambar. He has written and narrated the documentaries Havas-e Karbalā and Daʿwat, and has scripted television programmes including Pālīz and Ganjnāma.

Beyond Death

In early 2019 Aminikhah began a series of lectures for university students on Ān sūy-e marg, a Persian book of three near-death accounts. Over thirty-nine sessions he read the accounts and set them against the Qurʾān, the sayings of the Imams and the philosophy of Mullā Ṣadrā. Two question-and-answer sessions followed in 2020. In them he says the series grew far beyond the student circle he meant it for and was translated into Arabic.

He holds that the human being has a homeland beyond matter, that deeds take form in the barzakh, that wronging other people is the heaviest burden, that certainty is what such experiences are for, and that paradise itself is not the goal. His reading of NDEs sets out the argument, and NDEs in Iran gives the research background.
