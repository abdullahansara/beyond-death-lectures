Lectures on near-death accounts, 2019–2020

Beyond Death

In early 2019 a young Iranian cleric, Ḥujjat al-Islām Muṣṭafā Amīnīkhāh, spent about thirty-nine sessions with university students reading a Persian book of three near-death accounts, Ān sūy-e marg, and explaining what they mean in the light of the Qurʾān, the Imams' sayings and the philosophy of Mullā Ṣadrā. Two question-and-answer sessions followed in early 2020.

This site gathers the whole series: an English summary and the Persian transcript of every session, a link to each lecture's audio, and short essays on the speaker and on near-death experiences (NDEs) in Iran. Together the lectures are a long, candid statement of how a seminary-trained, Ṣadrian-leaning cleric with ties to the Leader's office understands NDEs.

The series

SessionsWhat they cover
1–5A woman who, in a coma after strokes and a cardiac arrest, is visited by a figure of light and recovers.
6–15An engineer who leaves his body twice, sees spirits and devils over his city, and meets his dead brother and mother.
16–39A university lecturer who passes through a tunnel to a valley where he must answer for wrongs to other people, and is given another chance after forgiving a debt.
40–41Questions and answers: sorcery, the rights of others, the appearance of the Imam of the Age and more.

Between the stories Amīnīkhāh teaches on the barzakh (the world between death and resurrection), the "imaginal world", the meaning of light, the rights of other people, and why the goal is God and not paradise.
